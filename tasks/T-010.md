## Goal

`DeviceListViewModel.refreshAll()` swallows every polling failure with `try?` and keeps the last
known `KsStatus`. A device that has dropped off the network therefore looks *fine* — it shows its
last bpm and peer count indefinitely, indistinguishable from a device that's live.

For an app whose whole job is telling you the state of hardware on stage, that is the wrong
default.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] `statuses` distinguishes fresh / stale / unreachable, rather than storing a bare `KsStatus?`
      (a small enum, or a status + `lastSeen` timestamp)
- [ ] The device list visibly marks a device that has missed N consecutive polls
- [ ] Recovery is automatic — a device that comes back clears the marker without user action
- [ ] The reboot after `POST /save` (T-007) and after an OTA flash (T-008) reads as an *expected*
      disappearance, not an error
- [ ] Written test-first per the /tdd rule: stub a failing session and assert the transition

## Notes

Don't just surface the error — decide the threshold. At a 2s poll, one dropped request on a busy
LAN is noise, not a disconnection; flapping a red badge on every missed packet is worse than the
current behaviour. Something like "3 consecutive failures" is the shape.

This one is load-bearing for T-007 and T-008: both deliberately reboot the device, and neither
has an honest way to show "it's coming back" until unreachability is a state the app can model.
